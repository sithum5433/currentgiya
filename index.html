NEIGHBOURS, NOT GUESSWORK

How the radar works

01

A report starts a signal

Reports need a location reading accurate to 200 metres. Selecting a town only changes your view. Browser locations are user-provided, not proof of presence.

02

Five independent neighbours verify it

Five different network addresses and devices within 1 km must report the same issue within 3 minutes. Shared Wi-Fi may count as one network.

03

A street or a wider area?

We group reports into 100-metre clusters. Spread across clusters suggests a wider outage. A single cluster suggests a street fault. This cannot identify an electrical phase or transformer.

04

Here for the outage. Gone afterwards.

Three independent restoration reports within 1 km clear the local power reports and chat. Reports otherwise expire after 2 hours. Utility account numbers stay in your browser. Your coordinates are used for nearby status, weather, reporting and chat. Public pins are rounded to about 100 metres. The server retains anonymous device and network hashes and report coordinates to prevent duplicates. Opted-in weather alerts save the selected coordinates and push subscription on the server.

Community verification is an estimate, not utility confirmation. HTTP checks measure this server’s responsiveness, not packet loss. Forecasts do not predict a power cut.
